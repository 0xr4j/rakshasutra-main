A last-heard
checkpoint.

Rakshasutra (SurakshaNet) is a proposed BLE-wearable and LoRaWAN checkpoint architecture for recording the most recent receiver observation along crowded pilgrim routes. It describes a checkpoint observation, not a live GPS location.

01 / Why this idea

Four everyday gaps.
One checkpoint question.

At a crowded shrine, a phone-based map may be unavailable or unwelcome. The proposed system asks a narrower question: “Which installed pole heard this band most recently?”

01

Phone dependence

Consumer trackers often depend on nearby participating phones and a working network path. This proposal places fixed receivers along selected routes.

Design motivation · check deployment assumptions
02

Gate-only reads

Passive RFID can be useful at a reader gate. A checkpoint network would aim to add observations between entry and exit points.

Concept comparison · not a universal RFID limitation
03

Signal shadows

Metal structures and dense crowds can weaken radio links. The paper proposes pole placement and antenna choices; the repo does not contain a site survey.

Open RF question · field measurements needed
04

Restricted phone use

Some queues or sanctum areas restrict phones. A wearable might still send a short signal, subject to local rules and consent.

Context-specific · verify with venue policy
?

The villain: blind spots

A band can only be “last heard” where a receiver is installed and the radio link succeeds. Between poles, the system has uncertainty.

One sentence to keep: last-heard is not live position.
→

Keep the claim small

This is a proposed checkpoint aid for authorized staff. It is not a substitute for emergency operations, GPS, or human care.

Research concept · not a safety product
02 / The proposed path

Four pieces.
One radio breadcrumb.

The concept separates the short wristband-to-pole radio hop from the longer pole-to-server backhaul. Each hop has to be built and tested.

◉
Wearable bandBroadcasts a short, changing BLE identifier. No coordinates are created by the band.
⌁
Smart poleListens nearby and records a time-stamped checkpoint observation when reception succeeds.
⇢
GatewayRelays compact pole messages over the proposed LoRaWAN backhaul.
▤
Authorized lookupA backend can resolve an observation for an approved safety workflow, under access controls.
Privacy promise to test, not an accomplished property: rotating IDs can reduce simple persistent tracking, but metadata, cryptographic details, retention, access control, and real-world privacy law still need independent review.

Scheduling changed the story

In the configured one-hour, 3,000-tag, 60-pole scenario over 40 deterministic seeds:

56.7%baseline packet loss
single arrivals + raw heartbeat
72.8%batched packet loss
15-second batch schedule
Despite fewer packets, narrow periodic batch windows clustered transmissions. Under these explicit assumptions the batched case lost more packets. This is a schedule-sensitivity warning, not a shrine deployment prediction.
How to read the bars: figures above come from the checked-in CSVs. The first pair are paper-input arithmetic. The second pair are generated synthetic output. Neither is a field measurement.

Safety, privacy, and intended use

This is an early research concept and analysis set. It is not a validated tracking product, emergency-response system, or safety-certified design. Do not rely on it for real-world safeguarding. Any prototype or deployment requires consent design, privacy/legal review, security testing, RF and battery testing, venue approval, and supervised operational trials.
